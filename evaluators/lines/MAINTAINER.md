# MAINTAINER

- __init__.py: exports `lines`, `Line`, `score_lines`, `expectations`, `no_vig`, `write_report`, `lines_table`, `expectations_table`.
- lines.py: `Line`, `lines` (game lines and props from `shared.lines`, results from the nflverse schedule and player stats), `no_vig`, `score_lines`, `expectations` (and `_covariance`), `favorites` (moneylines from the favorite's side, for the reliability table), and `PROPS` (the prop markets: `shared.lines.STATS`' stat names). A prop's real value is `shared.lines.value` of the player's row in `shared.nflverse.stat_lines`.
- report.py: `write_report`, `lines_table`, and `expectations_table`.
- __main__.py: the CLI.
- test_lines.py: `no_vig`; `score_lines` on made-up lines and samples; `expectations` on a simulator that makes half of what it knows; the cached season. The last is skipped if the caches are missing.

Notes:
- nflverse's `spread_line` is the points the home team is favored by and `result` is home minus away, so the home team covers when `result > spread_line`.
- A push (the number landing exactly on the line) isn't scored.
- `expectations` asks the question the game rows' scores answer only slowly: does the simulator tell the teams apart, and by enough? The mean of 50 sims of a game is itself off by about 1.8 points, as much as the expectations differ between games, so the spread is taken from the covariance of the two halves' means, whose noise is independent, and the per-point values divide by it. That takes the bias out, not the noise: `se` is each measure's standard error from drawing the games again 200 times (`RESAMPLES`), and at 480 games and 50 sims a spread is good to about 0.13 points, a correlation to 0.05, and the line's points per simulated point to 0.2. More sims sharpen it faster than more games do. On 480 games of 2021 to 2026 (2026-11-17) the margin's expectation had a spread of 2.27 points against the line's 5.84, a correlation of 0.71 with it, and 1.8 points of line per simulated point; the total's 1.93 against 4.36, 0.61, and 1.4. A day of work on who is playing had moved the margin's spread from 1.89 (by 0.38, standard error 0.16) and no game row's score.
- A prop whose player has no stat line for the game is left out. That drops players who didn't play (a void bet), and also the few who played without recording any stat (which would have gone under).
- A prop with both prices is scored at their no-vig probability (2022 to 2025); one without (all of 2026) at 50%. A juiced line (receptions 1.5 at -160) isn't really a median, so `over` for an unpriced market can sit a few points off 50%, and the markets on rare events sit far off it (a sack line of 0.5 goes over 30% of the time, an interception line 8%).
- To add a prop market: add ESPN's name to `shared.lines.STATS` (and, for a prop on a sum of stats, its weights to `shared.lines.COMBINED`), and the stat's nflverse columns to `shared.nflverse.STAT_COLUMNS` if they aren't there. Stat names are the play scenario's (`scenarios/play/stats.py`), so a simulated stat line can be looked up by the same name.
