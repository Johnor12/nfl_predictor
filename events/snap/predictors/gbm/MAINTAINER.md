# MAINTAINER

- __init__.py: `predict` (the baseline's, with this package's `pass_rate`) and `pass_rate` (the season's model on a scrimmage down; the baseline's on a try, or when the season has no model).
- inputs.py: `NAMES` and `features(ctx)`, the one place the model's inputs are read, for training and for predicting; `_game` reads the offense's game so far from the history.
- train.py: `example` (a real play as a training row), `train(season)`, and the CLI.
- model_<season>.txt: the model for that season's games (LightGBM's text format, about 0.5 MB), trained on 2011 up to the season before.
- test_gbm.py: the model reads the situation; synthetic plays stay valid; the fallback before the first model; the history features.

Measured (2026-11-15, the evaluators' default 160 games of 2021 to 2026 at 100 sims, against the baseline case by case; it became the snap event's default on that day, and the baseline's reports are kept next to the current ones as `evaluation_output/*_baseline.md`):
- Play evaluator: `snap: action` 0.3845 -> 0.3544 (-7.8%, 18 standard errors; skill +19.5% -> +25.8%), `play: action (scrimmage)` 0.5312 -> 0.5099 (-4.0%), `play: yards (scrimmage)` -0.2%. Nothing else moved.
- Game evaluator: no better on the scores. The game and team rows are all within 2 standard errors; of the player rows, the first quarterback's attempts (-1.9%) and completions (-1.7%) and the second back's carries (-2.0%) are better, and the first quarterback's rushing yards (+2.3%) and the first tight end's catches (+2.5%) and yards (+1.7%) are worse. What did change is the spread of a game's runs and passes, which was too narrow: the real value fell in the bottom or top tenth of `team: rush_att`'s forecast 31% of the time before and 16% after (`team: pass_att` 31% -> 24%, the first quarterback's attempts 34% -> 26%).
- So the model knows better what a team does in a given spot, but not which teams run or pass more over a whole game: that takes inputs about the team (below), not about the play.

Notes:
- Out of sample: a season's model has seen nothing of that season, and the features and settings were chosen on 2019 and 2020 with models trained on 2011 to 2018, before any evaluated season. One choice was made on evaluated games: leaving the formation out (below).
- What each input is worth, on the 192,588 real runs and dropbacks of 2021 to 2026, each season scored by its own model (two-class Brier, the evaluators' `snap: action`): the model scores 0.3516, against 0.4773 for the overall pass rate. Without the game so far, the situation alone, 0.3577 (+1.7%).
- The betting lines were tested and left out. With the points the offense is favored by and the total as two more features the model scores 0.3508 (0.24% better, 5.7 standard errors, better in 5 of the 6 seasons). That gain is the market's own forecast of the game (a favorite is expected to lead, and to run), which the root README says not to borrow. Putting them back is two names and two values in `inputs.py` (`Game.spread`, `Game.total`), a deleted table cache, and a retrain.
- Tried and left out, for no gain: the roof, wind, and temperature; the offense's season before the game (its share of passes and its quarterbacks' runs, summed from its active players' `Player.stats`: +0.0%).
- The formation, the personnel group, and the box are left out on purpose. On real plays they are worth another 5.8% (shotgun is most of it). But in a simulated play they come from the lineups and formation baselines, which know less about the play than this model does, so the model was conditioning on a formation that didn't fit the rest of its inputs: on 48 games at 30 sims, `snap: action` improved 5.5% on the baseline with them, 6.1% without the box, and 7.5% with none of them. They belong back in (the `formation`, `lineups`, and `defense` arguments are there for it) once the offensive formation predictor is a trained one that reads what this one reads; that is the natural next model.
- Not tried: the coaches (`Game.home_coach`, `away_coach`) and a team's tendency beyond this game (its pass rate over what the situations called for in earlier games). Neither fits in a count, and the context has no team-level history to read them from.
- The settings are `shared.models`' defaults (150 trees of 31 leaves, learning rate 0.1). On the 2019 and 2020 plays: twice the trees at half the rate scored 0.1% better for twice the file; 63 leaves, 15 leaves, other leaf sizes, and weighting recent seasons more were all within 0.3%.
- The history features only work if a simulated game's history says what a real one's does: `scenarios.play.play_summary` and `shared/nflverse/contexts.py`'s `_summary` must give a play the same `offense`, `kind`, and `yards`. The play evaluator can't see a mismatch (its histories are real); the game evaluator's `team: pass_att` and `team: rush_att` rows would.
- Spikes, kneels, and two-point plays aren't the model's: the baseline's hand-set rules and counts decide those first, and only real runs and dropbacks on scrimmage downs are trained on.
- After changing `inputs.py` or `example`, delete `.cache/training/snap_gbm_*.parquet` (the table is cached per season and never rebuilt on its own) and retrain every model.
- Retraining gives the same file from the same table. A season in progress isn't trained on: its model stays the one from before it started.
