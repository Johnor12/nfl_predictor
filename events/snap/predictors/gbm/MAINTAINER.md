# MAINTAINER

- __init__.py: `predict` (the baseline's, with this package's `pass_rate`) and `pass_rate` (the season's model on a scrimmage down; the baseline's on a try, or when the season has no model).
- inputs.py: `NAMES` and `features(ctx, group, shotgun, box)`, the one place the model's inputs are read, for training and for predicting: `events.situation`'s (the situation, and the offense's game so far from the history), plus the personnel group, the shotgun, and the box.
- train.py: `example` (a real play as a training row), `train(season)`, `TABLES`, and the CLI.
- model_<season>.txt: the model for that season's games (LightGBM's text format, about 0.5 MB), trained on 2011 up to the season before.
- test_gbm.py: the model reads the situation and the formation; synthetic plays stay valid; the fallback before the first model; the history and personnel features.

Measured (2026-11-15, the evaluators' default 160 games of 2021 to 2026 at 100 sims, against the baseline case by case; it became the snap event's default on that day, and the baseline's reports are kept next to the current ones as `evaluation_output/*_baseline.md`):
- Play evaluator: `snap: action` 0.3845 -> 0.3544 (-7.8%, 18 standard errors; skill +19.5% -> +25.8%), `play: action (scrimmage)` 0.5312 -> 0.5099 (-4.0%), `play: yards (scrimmage)` -0.2%. Nothing else moved.
- Game evaluator: no better on the scores. The game and team rows are all within 2 standard errors; of the player rows, the first quarterback's attempts (-1.9%) and completions (-1.7%) and the second back's carries (-2.0%) are better, and the first quarterback's rushing yards (+2.3%) and the first tight end's catches (+2.5%) and yards (+1.7%) are worse. What did change is the spread of a game's runs and passes, which was too narrow: the real value fell in the bottom or top tenth of `team: rush_att`'s forecast 31% of the time before and 16% after (`team: pass_att` 31% -> 24%, the first quarterback's attempts 34% -> 26%).
- So the model knows better what a team does in a given spot, but not which teams run or pass more over a whole game: that takes inputs about the team (below), not about the play.

Measured again with the personnel group, the shotgun, and the box as inputs, and the trained offensive formation predictor in front of it (2026-11-15, the same 160 games at 100 sims, against the system before both, case by case; that system's reports are kept as `evaluation_output/*_before_formation.md`):
- Play evaluator, the snap told how the offense really lined up: `snap: action` 0.3544 -> 0.3297 (-7.0%, 15 standard errors; skill +25.8% -> +31.0%). The play evaluator hands the snap the real play's shotgun, personnel group, and box from that day on (`evaluators/play/README.md`); the model before read none of them, so its row is the same either way.
- Play evaluator, end to end: `play: action (scrimmage)` 0.5099 -> 0.5107 (+0.15%, 1.6 standard errors), `play: yards (scrimmage)` +0.06% (1.9). No better, and no worse beyond noise.
- Game evaluator: nothing. Every game and team row is within 1.1 standard errors; of the 33 player rows, three are beyond 2 (the first quarterback's rushing yards -2.4%, the second receiver's catches +1.5% and yards +1.6%).
- That is what should happen. In a simulated play the formation comes from a model that knows what this one knows, so reading it tells this model nothing new: the chance of a pass is the same, now split into shotgun or not and then run or pass. The 7% is there when the formation is known from outside the simulation, and the plays are now consistent (a pass comes from the shotgun as often as real ones do), which is what anything downstream that reads the formation will need. An end-to-end gain needs an earlier event that knows something this model doesn't: which team it is (below).

Tried with the team's pass tendency and left as it was (2026-11-15; the model files are the ones from before):
- The input: how often the team dropped back on its neutral plays (early downs of a one-score game, outside the fourth quarter and a half's last two minutes), this season before the game and over the earlier seasons, read coarsely (`shared/models/README.md`).
- To a model that isn't told the formation it is worth something: 0.3% per play over 2013 to 2020, each season scored by a model of the others (10 standard errors), and 2% on a team's dropbacks over a game (2 standard errors). To this model, which is told the formation, it was worth nothing on the evaluated seasons: `snap: action` 0.3286 -> 0.3277 on the evaluators' 160 games (within noise), and on every run and dropback of 2021 to 2026, each season scored by a model of the seasons before it, 0.0% per play with a team's dropbacks over a game 3.5% further off (3.3 standard errors). Read exactly it was worse on both.
- Worse still everywhere: the team's overall pass rate (it is mostly the scores of its games), its points for and against, the defense's rates. The head coach's rate was the same as his team's.
- Why nothing, when teams do differ: a team that passes a lot lines up in the shotgun a lot, and the formation model now reads the team's shotgun rate (`events/offensive_formation/predictors/gbm/MAINTAINER.md`). This model is told the formation, and reads the offense's dropbacks and runs in this game besides, so a team's habits have already reached it twice.

Notes:
- Out of sample: a season's model has seen nothing of that season, and the features and settings were chosen on 2019 and 2020 with models trained on 2011 to 2018, before any evaluated season (there the shotgun was worth 5.5% and the personnel group another 0.5%). The box couldn't be: it is only charted from 2022 on, so it was judged on evaluated games.
- What each input is worth, on the 192,588 real runs and dropbacks of 2021 to 2026, each season scored by its own model on the real inputs (two-class Brier, the evaluators' `snap: action`), against 0.4773 for the overall pass rate: the situation alone 0.3577; with the game so far 0.3516; with the shotgun 0.3314 (-5.7%); with the personnel group too 0.3308 (-5.9%; the group alone, without the shotgun, -0.9%); with the box 0.3272 (-6.9%).
- The same models with the formation taken from the shotgun model instead of the real play (each play's pass rate from the shotgun, weighted by the shotgun model's probability, plus the same from under center): 0.3516, exactly the model without the formation. That is the offline version of the end-to-end result above.
- The betting lines were tested and left out. With the points the offense is favored by and the total as two more features the model scores 0.3508 (0.24% better, 5.7 standard errors, better in 5 of the 6 seasons). That gain is the market's own forecast of the game (a favorite is expected to lead, and to run), which the root README says not to borrow. Putting them back is two names and two values in `inputs.py` (`Game.spread`, `Game.total`), a deleted table cache, and a retrain.
- Tried and left out, for no gain: the roof, wind, and temperature; the offense's season before the game (its share of passes and its quarterbacks' runs, summed from its active players' `Player.stats`: +0.0%).
- The formation only works as an input with the trained offensive formation predictor in front (`events/offensive_formation/predictors/gbm/`). With the formation baseline, which draws the shotgun from down, distance, and personnel alone, the model was conditioning on a formation that didn't fit the rest of its inputs, and scored worse end to end than without it: on 48 games at 30 sims it improved on the snap baseline by 5.5% with the three inputs and by 7.5% with none. With the trained predictor, on the same 48 games, `play: action (scrimmage)` against the model without the inputs: +0.07% with all three, +0.11% without the box, +0.02% with the shotgun alone (all within one standard error). So `--swap offensive_formation=baseline` alone makes the snap worse; swap both.
- The personnel group and the box still come from baselines that know little about the play (`lineups`' count by game script, down, and distance; `defensive_formation`'s by down and distance). Neither cost anything measurable end to end, but neither can add anything there until its own predictor reads the situation. The box is the weaker of the two: a trained box predictor that reads the shotgun and the personnel group is the next model of this kind (it needs more than a yes/no output from `shared.models`).
- Tried and left out: the defense's package (its defensive backs), worth another 0.2% on real plays.
- Not read: the team or its coach beyond this game (see above for what was tried).
- The settings are `shared.models`' defaults (150 trees of 31 leaves, learning rate 0.1). On the 2019 and 2020 plays: twice the trees at half the rate scored 0.1% better for twice the file; 63 leaves, 15 leaves, other leaf sizes, and weighting recent seasons more were all within 0.3%.
- The history features (`events/situation.py`) only work if a simulated game's history says what a real one's does: `scenarios.play.play_summary` and `shared/nflverse/contexts.py`'s `_summary` must give a play the same `offense`, `kind`, and `yards`. The play evaluator can't see a mismatch (its histories are real); the game evaluator's `team: pass_att` and `team: rush_att` rows would.
- Spikes, kneels, and two-point plays aren't the model's: the baseline's hand-set rules and counts decide those first, and only real runs and dropbacks on scrimmage downs are trained on.
- After changing `inputs.py` or `example`, delete `.cache/training/snap_gbm_*.parquet` (the table is cached per season and never rebuilt on its own) and retrain every model. `events/situation.py` is shared with the offensive formation model: after changing it, do the same for both.
- Retraining gives the same file from the same table. A season in progress isn't trained on: its model stays the one from before it started.
