# nflverse

Real NFL data from [nflverse](https://github.com/nflverse/nflverse-data), cached locally and converted into play and game scenario inputs.

API (`from shared.nflverse import ...`):

- `SEASONS`: the seasons the system covers: 2011 (the first the rulebook has) through the current one.
- `load(name, season) -> DataFrame`: a cached file, or None where nflverse has none for the season: `pbp`, `ftn` (charting, 2022 on), `participation` (who was on the field for each play, with personnel groups; 2016 on, published after the season), `snap_counts` (each player's snaps per game, 2013 on), `rosters` (weekly), `depth_charts`, `injuries`, `player_stats` (every player's box score per game), `pfr_def`, `pfr_pass`, `pfr_rush`, `pfr_rec` (Pro Football Reference's charting of each player's game, 2018 on), `players` (one row per player with his ids in every system), `games` (schedule with weather, coaches, referees, betting lines), `play_callers` (who called each team's offensive and defensive plays in each game, with its head coach: Sam Hoppen's file, the one source here that isn't nflverse's). Downloads into `.cache/nflverse/` (gitignored) on first use. A file that can still change (the current season's, `games`, `players`, `play_callers`) is downloaded again once its cached copy is 6 hours old, so the cache never needs refreshing by hand; a past season's stays as it is unless `load(name, season, refresh=True)`. Offline, the cached copy is used.
- `stat_lines(season) -> DataFrame`: what every player did in every game, one row per game and player (a game is in once its box score and, from 2018 on, its charting are both published, a few days after it's played): `game_id`, `week`, `player_id`, `team`, and a column per stat in the play scenario's names. This is the truth a simulated stat line is compared with, for every position (see "every player" below).
- `real_plays(season, weeks=None, games=None) -> list[RealPlay]`: every real snap or free kick, in game order (`weeks` and `games`, a list of game ids, each narrow it down). `RealPlay.context` is a valid `PlayContext`, `RealPlay.actual` a dict of what happened (action, yards, points by team, touchdown, turnover, penalty, kick results, the real game seconds to the next snap and since the previous one (`seconds_to_next`, `seconds_since_snap`), `timeout`: the team that called one before the play, `timeout_second`: the other team, if it then called one too, and `challenge`: `(team, won)` for a coach's challenge of the play), and `RealPlay.row` the pbp row index.
- `real_samples(season, weeks=None, games=None)`: iterates the same plays as `shared.priors.Sample`s: the context and `actual`, plus the play's nflverse rows (`row`, the pbp row; `charting`, the FTN row or None; `participation`, who was on the field, or None), each a plain object with the file's columns as attributes (`row.yards_gained`). For code that reads what an event's output was: the priors builders and `evaluators/play`.
- `real_games(season, weeks=None, games=None) -> list[RealGame]`: every game in the schedule, played or not. `RealGame.context` is a `GameContext` with the same game info and active players as the game's real plays. `RealGame.actual` is what happened, or None if the game hasn't been played: `points` by team, `margin` (home minus away), `total`, `winner`, `overtime`, `plays`, `teams` (`{team: {stat: value}}`), and `players` (`{player id: {stat: value}}`), with the play scenario's stat names. The same keys as `simulators.game.summarize`.
- `STAT_COLUMNS`: the box score stats, each with the nflverse player stats columns it is the sum of. `CHARTED`: the charted stats, by PFR file; `CHARTED_SINCE`: the first season that has them (2018).
- `TEAM_STATS`: the stats in a team's, coach's, or referee's form. `ON_FIELD`: the ones also counted for each player on the field.

Inputs filled from data: game info (roof, surface, weather (the temperature and wind of an outdoor game, from the schedule, or from the play-by-play's weather report where the schedule has none; none indoors), both coaching staffs (see below), referee, neutral site, the betting lines: the closing spread and total, or the latest ones for a game that hasn't been played, and the form of both teams, their head coaches and play callers, and the referee: see below); active players (weekly roster status minus injury-report Outs, plus anyone who appears in the game's play-by-play) with position, jersey, depth chart slots from the last snapshot before kickoff (in a played game the quarterback who started is first at QB, whatever that snapshot said), experience, draft pick, season-to-date stats from earlier weeks (everything recorded about him, whatever his position: see "every player" below), the same stats over earlier seasons (`career_stats`), and recent snaps; the situation (kind, possession, spot, down and distance, score, quarter and clock, timeouts, and `fair_catch_kick` on the down after a fair catch); the game's earlier plays as `history`; and `warned`, the players already flagged for unsportsmanlike conduct in the game.

## every player

Every player on the field has a real stat line per game (`stat_lines`, and `RealGame.actual["players"]`) and a history before each game (`Player.stats` for the season so far, `Player.career` for earlier seasons). Both use the same names. A stat a player has none of is absent.

| what | stats | seasons |
|---|---|---|
| box score: passing | `pass_att`, `pass_cmp`, `pass_yds`, `pass_td`, `int_thrown`, `sacked`, `sack_yards` (lost), `pass_air_yds`, `pass_yac`, `pass_first_downs` | 2011 on |
| box score: rushing, receiving | `rush_att`, `rush_yds`, `rush_td`, `rush_first_downs`, `targets`, `receptions`, `rec_yds`, `rec_td`, `target_air_yds`, `rec_yac`, `rec_first_downs`, `two_pt`, `fumbles`, `fumbles_lost` | 2011 on |
| box score: defense | `tackles` (solo), `assists`, `tfl`, `sacks`, `qb_hits`, `interceptions`, `int_ret_yds`, `passes_defended`, `forced_fumbles`, `fumble_recoveries`, `fumble_ret_yds`, `fumble_ret_td`, `kicks_blocked`, `safeties`, `def_td` (an interception returned) | 2011 on |
| box score: kicking and returns | `fg_att`, `fg_made`, `fg_made_yds` (the made kicks' distances, summed), `xp_att`, `xp_made`, `punts`, `punt_yds`, `punt_returns`, `punt_ret_yds`, `kick_returns`, `kick_ret_yds`, `special_teams_td` | 2011 on |
| box score: flags | `penalties`, `penalty_yards` | 2011 on |
| charted, defense | `pressures`, `hurries`, `blitzes`, `missed_tackles`, and in coverage `coverage_targets`, `coverage_cmp`, `coverage_yds`, `coverage_td` | 2018 on |
| charted, offense | a passer's `pressured`, `hurried`, `hit`, `blitzed`, `bad_throws`, `pass_drops` (by his receivers); a runner's `rush_yds_before_contact`, `rush_yds_after_contact`, `rush_broken_tackles`; a receiver's `drops`, `rec_broken_tackles` | 2018 on |
| usage (history only) | `carries` (designed runs), `dropbacks`, `scrambles`, `times_sacked`, `pass_cmp_over` (a passer's completions over what nflverse's `cp` expected of his throws) | 2011 on |
| snaps (history only) | `offense_snaps`, `defense_snaps`, `special_teams_snaps` | 2013 on (1% of players unmatched) |
| on the field (history only) | `on_<stat>`: what his team's offense did on the plays he was on the field for it; `on_allowed_<stat>`: what the other offense did on the plays he was on the field against it. `<stat>` is one of `ON_FIELD`: plays, dropbacks, runs, pass_att, pass_cmp, pass_yds, int_thrown, sacked, scrambles, rush_yds, dropback_yds, first_downs, touchdowns, fumbles_lost, pressures | 2016 to 2025 (2026 once nflverse publishes its participation file) |

- The on-field stats are what linemen have instead of a box score: a tackle's `on_sacked / on_dropbacks` and `on_pressures / on_dropbacks` are his line's sack and pressure rates with him on the field, and `on_rush_yds / on_runs` its yards per run. What happened without him is his team's form less his own (`game.form(team).stats["sacked"] - player.stats["on_sacked"]`); `pressures` is the one stat a team's form doesn't have.
- The box score's `tackles` are solo tackles, as a box score and a sportsbook count them: alone, or as the tackler another player assisted.
- The play scenario credits every stat in the box score and charted rows (`scenarios/play/stats.py`), so each has a simulated value to compare with; the game evaluator scores them all. It also credits `kickoffs`, `kickoff_touchbacks`, `fair_catches`, and the return touchdowns by kind (`int_ret_td`, `punt_ret_td`, `kick_ret_td`), which the box score doesn't break out.
- How the box score defines them, checked against the play-by-play: a sack is also a tackle (an assist each when shared); `tfl` is a run or a catch stopped behind the line, and a sack made alone for a loss; `def_td` is only an interception returned for a touchdown (a fumble returned is `fumble_ret_td`, and a punt, kickoff, or blocked kick returned is `special_teams_td`); `pass_air_yds` counts every attempt and `target_air_yds` every target; the box score's sack yards are negative and ours are the yards lost.
- How the charting defines them, from its totals: a passer's `pressured` is `hurried` + `hit` + `sacked`, where `hit` is a hit without a sack (about half the official `qb_hits`, which count sacks); a defender's `pressures` are his hurries, hits, and sacks; nearly every target is charted against a defender (`coverage_targets`), and what he allowed adds up to the passing game; `rush_yds_before_contact` + `rush_yds_after_contact` = `rush_yds`; the charters count about twice as many `missed_tackles` as broken ones.
- Not in any free source: who blocked whom, the sacks and pressures a lineman himself allowed, who rushed the passer, and who covered whom on a play.

Coaching staffs (`game.staff(team)`, a `Staff`; `shared/nflverse/staff.py`): for every team and game from 2011 on, played or not, the head coach, the offensive and defensive coordinators, and who called the plays on each side of the ball, by name. The play callers and head coaches come from the play-caller file, which follows the season in progress. The coordinators are ours (`staff.csv`, a row per stint with its source), since nobody publishes them: 40 of the 1,024 team-seasons-and-sides have nobody with the title (a head coach who runs that side himself), and 5 have two (`"Brad Childress / Matt Nagy"`). The head coach calls the offense in 36% of games and the defense in 14%; a coordinator who doesn't call the plays is in the inputs by name only. The schedule's head coaches miss some interim coaches (every one of 2024 and 2025), so `Staff.head_coach` is the one to read.

Form, who is playing (`Game.forms`, read with `game.form(who)`; `shared/nflverse/form.py`): what each team, coach, and referee did before the game, as totals of `TEAM_STATS`, in two windows: this season's earlier games (`Form.stats`) and the earlier seasons back to 2011, each counting half the one after it (`Form.career`). A team's stats are its offense's (its plays, dropbacks, runs, shotgun snaps, passes, completions, yards, its dropbacks and completions over what nflverse's own models expected of its situations and throws (`dropbacks_over`, `pass_cmp_over`: what a rating is made of), sacks, scrambles, first downs, touchdowns, fumbles lost, its snaps and the flags on it and on the defense, its fourth downs and how many it went for), the same under `allowed_<stat>` for what its defense allowed, and `games`, `points`, `allowed_points`. A coach's form (`game.form(game.staff(team).offensive_play_caller)`) holds each stat from the games he was the one deciding it: the offense's stats from the games he called its plays, the defense's (`allowed_<stat>`) from the games he called its plays, and the games, points, and fourth downs from the games he was head coach. It is one form per coach, whatever his jobs, and it follows him to a new team and a new job. A referee's is both offenses' stats in the games he worked. A player's `career_stats` are discounted the same way. Nothing in them comes from the game itself or from later ones. A rate is a division away (`events/strength.py`).

On-field personnel, from the best source each season has: a player's `snaps` are his snaps over the four most recent weeks he played in before this one, by unit (the weeks he missed don't count against him). A season with a participation file (2016 to 2025) counts them play by play in the play scenario's units (`offense`, `defense`, `punt`, `punt_return`, `field_goal`, `field_goal_block`, `kickoff`, `kick_return`). A season without one (2013 to 2015, and 2026 until nflverse publishes it) uses the per-game snap counts, which only say `offense`, `defense`, or `special_teams`. 2011 and 2012 have neither, and nobody has snaps.

A context is the state after any timeout before the play: the timeout is already charged and the clock stopped. `shared.priors.timeout_case` rebuilds the state before it. The timeout a lost challenge costs is charged after the challenged play, as the game scenario does it, and isn't a timeout before the next one.

A game that hasn't been played uses each team's latest weekly roster, without the game-day inactives, so its inputs are rougher (and sometimes unusable: no kicker activated yet).

Inferred, not recorded: whether the clock was running at the snap and the play clock (from the previous play), and safety kicks (the free kick after a safety).

Every season comes out in the same shape, whatever nflverse's files looked like that year:

- Teams have their current codes everywhere (the 2015 St. Louis Rams are `LA`, as in the play-by-play); a game id keeps the code of its day (`2015_01_SEA_STL`).
- Positions are the play scenario's (`OL`, `DL`, `LB`, `DB`); the rosters before 2016 list tackles, ends, and corners.
- Depth chart slots have the names nflverse uses from 2025 on (`PK`, `KR`, `LCB`). Before 2025 the charts are weekly and in the teams' own words; the common slots are renamed, the rest are kept as they are. They also run a week or two behind what teams actually did.
- From 2016 to 2018 the weekly rosters don't mark game-day inactives, so a team's active players are everyone on the roster who isn't ruled out on the injury report (about 53 instead of 46 to 48).
