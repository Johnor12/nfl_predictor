# MAINTAINER

- __init__.py: exports `SEASONS`, `load`, `real_plays`, `real_samples`, `RealPlay`, `real_games`, `RealGame`, `stat_lines`, `STAT_COLUMNS`, `CHARTED`, `ON_FIELD`, `TEAM_STATS`.
- download.py: `SEASONS`, `load`, and the file URLs (`FILES`); caches under `.cache/nflverse/`. A file in `OPTIONAL` that isn't published (404) leaves a `.missing` marker, so it's only asked for once. `load` renames the teams that have moved (`TEAMS`) in every file's team columns.
- depth.py: `depth_charts(season)`: the depth charts in the 2025+ shape for every season (the weekly charts before 2025 are dated the day before the week's first game, and their slots renamed by `SLOTS`).
- contexts.py: `real_samples` (and `_rows`, which hands a file's rows over as plain objects), and `real_plays` and its pipeline (`_Rosters`, `_game`, `_kind`, `_clock_state`, `_situation`, `_actual`, `_summary`, `_seconds_to_next`).
- stat_lines.py: `stat_lines`: every player's real stat line per game in our stat names, from the box score (`STAT_COLUMNS`) and PFR's charting (`CHARTED`, by gsis id through the `players` file).
- form.py: the tallies of what was done before a game: everything recorded about a player by week (`weekly_stats`: his usage from the play-by-play (`USAGE`), his stat lines, his snaps, and what happened while he was on the field (`ON_FIELD`)), his running totals (`Totals`), his snaps by unit (`weekly_snaps`), and `Forms`: the form of a season's teams, coaches, and referees game by game (`TEAM_STATS`), with every earlier season's totals discounted and added (`_totals`, cached per season in `.cache/form/`, and `_earlier`).
- games.py: `real_games`, `RealGame`, and `_actual` (a game's real outcome from the schedule and the stat lines). Uses contexts.py's `_Rosters`, `_players`, and `_game`, so a game and its plays get the same inputs.
- test_form.py: a team's season builds up game by game; earlier seasons are discounted; coaches and referees; a game not played yet. Skipped if the cache is missing.
- test_stat_lines.py: the stat lines' shape and consistency; every position's history before a game; the on-field totals against the team's. Skipped if the cache is missing.
- test_contexts.py: week 1 contexts are valid; conventions (kickoff orientation, try spots, clock inference); timeouts before plays; challenges and the down after a fair catch; week 1 games (inputs, outcomes, stat lines); a season in the older formats (2014). Skipped if the cache is missing.

Notes:
- nflverse lists the receiving team as `posteam` on kickoffs, so the kicking team is our offense there and `x = yardline_100`.
- A try's `x` is the two-point spot. A kicked try is snapped 13 yards behind it, so penalty-moved kicks map back (clamped at the 1 for the rare kick snapped inside the 13).
- The pre-play score is the previous row's `total_home_score`/`total_away_score` (those columns are post-play).
- Weekly rosters can lag the current week's game-day inactives (week 9 had none marked when built), so a few contexts carry extra players.
- 2026 participation data (who was on the field, personnel, formation) isn't published yet; 2016 to 2025 are. When it appears, delete `.cache/nflverse/pbp_participation_2026.missing` (or `load("participation", 2026, refresh=True)`) and rebuild the 2026 priors: the snaps switch to the per-unit counts and the lineups priors gain 2026's personnel on their own.
- In a participation row `offense_players` is the team nflverse lists in possession, which on a kickoff is the receiving team. `_weekly_snaps` names each side's unit from the play type (`UNITS`); `shared.priors.played` splits `players_on_play` by team instead.
- Snap counts come from Pro Football Reference, with its own player ids. The weekly rosters carry a PFR id for about 80% of the rows (few linemen), the rest are matched by team and name; about 1% stay unmatched and have no snaps.
- Form costs a season's load 0.15 seconds, and the first load of a season about a second for each earlier season (their totals are then in `.cache/form/<season>.parquet`). A cached season is never rebuilt on its own: delete `.cache/form/` after changing what `weekly_stats` or `TEAM_STATS` count (a rebuild takes about 40 seconds), and a season's file after refreshing that season's play-by-play. The season being played is never cached (its games are counted from its play-by-play every time).
- A new team stat is a name in `TEAM_STATS` and a column in `_counted` (how one play of the offense counts toward it); the defense's `allowed_` twin, the coaches', and the referees' follow. A new player stat is a column in `STAT_COLUMNS` or `CHARTED` if a file has it per game (it is then in the stat lines and in every player's history), else a row in `USAGE` (counted from the play-by-play, history only). The team's stats are a box score of its offense, which is more than the trained predictors read so far (the shotgun, yards per run, fourth downs gone for, and the completions and interceptions allowed); a stat made for one input (the pass rate on neutral downs) went out again when the input did.
- The earlier seasons count half each (`DECAY`, the same as the priors'). A model is given a rate from each window and can weigh them itself, so the number only decides how the seasons before this one are mixed.
- A referee has one row per game (both offenses summed); a team and a coach have one each per game. Counting a referee's two team rows separately would put the other team's half of the same game into "before this game".
- The schedule's temperature and wind are missing for many outdoor games of recent seasons (91 of 198 in 2022, 41 in 2023, about half of 2026 so far; 2% before 2021). `_weather` fills them from the play-by-play's weather report ("Sunny Temp: 73° F, Humidity: 79%, Wind: W 3 mph"), which every played game has. Without it a model that reads the weather sees an outdoor game with none, which in the seasons it was trained on meant a dome. Where both exist they agree to the degree about 80% of the time (they are two sources). A game that hasn't been played has neither until the schedule file gets a forecast.
- A player's history has four sources (`weekly_stats`). The passing, rushing, receiving, and punting stats are counted from the play-by-play (`USAGE`), not read from the box score, because the predictors were built on those counts; every other stat comes from his stat lines. The snaps are `weekly_snaps` summed over the season's earlier weeks: nobody has them in 2011 and 2012, or the 1% of players the snap counts can't be matched to, and a predictor reading `offense_snaps` falls back on the player's role. `rush_att` counts a quarterback's scrambles and kneels, as the box score does; `carries` doesn't.
- The history is kept as one table of running totals per season (`Totals`), and a player's tuple is built when a game asks for it: building every player's for every week took 6 seconds a season. With it a season's load costs about 0.5 seconds more than before the defenders' and linemen's stats were added (the on-field totals split every play's player lists a second time; `weekly_snaps` does it first).
- The on-field totals count scrimmage plays only (`_counted`'s `plays`: runs and dropbacks, no kneels, spikes, or two-point tries), so a player's `on_plays` is a little less than his `offense_snaps`. `pressures` is the participation file's `was_pressure`, which every season of it has on dropbacks.
- PFR's ids are matched through nflverse's `players` file, which has every one of them (the snap counts are still matched through the weekly rosters and names, which leaves 1% out: switching them would change the snaps predictors read, so it needs a before and after). A PFR id can also belong to a player from before 1999 with an old-style id; only the `00-` ids are used.
- A charting file can be a week ahead of the cached box scores (they are refreshed separately); a game is in the stat lines once its box score is.
- The box score's `sacks` include half sacks. Before the stat lines, a player's history counted only the sacks he had alone; no predictor read it.
- `SNAP_WEEKS` (4) is how far back a player's snaps reach. Two to four weeks predicted 2025's lineups best (see `events/lineups/predictors/baseline/MAINTAINER.md`).
- `_actual` and `simulators.play.summarize` must produce the same keys with the same meaning (the audit and `evaluators/play` compare them). Change them together.
- `games._actual` and `simulators.game.summarize` must produce the same keys with the same meaning (`evaluators/game` compares them). Change them together.
- Timeouts are their own pbp rows (`timeout == 1`, `timeout_team`). The first between two plays is the next play's `timeout`, and a different team's after it `timeout_second`. A play's own row has `timeout == 1` when its challenge was lost, and its timeout columns are already one short: `real_plays` gives that timeout back to the challenged play's context.
- A challenge is read from the play's description (`_challenge`): "Carolina challenged the pass completion ruling, and the play was REVERSED." `CITIES` maps the names descriptions use (San Diego, St. Louis, Oakland included) to team codes; every season from 2011 parses, with no name outside the two teams playing. The replay official's reviews (`replay_or_challenge == 1` without a team) aren't challenges.
- The player stats file has a leftover row per team without a player id (penalties that weren't assigned). `games._actual` skips it, so team penalties are the players' only.
- `real_plays` and `real_games` take 1 to 2 seconds for a season before the first play or game comes out, nearly all of it building the rosters (`_Rosters`, the same for one game as for the whole season); that is the fixed cost of every evaluator process. What is per game (`real_games`' stat lines and play counts) is only read for the games asked for.
- A sample's rows are plain objects, not pandas Series: reading an attribute of a Series takes about 20 microseconds, and the priors builders and the play evaluator read dozens per play (the evaluator once per simulated run). The values are the Series' own (numpy numbers, `nan` where missing).
- A cached file doesn't refresh itself: `load(name, season, refresh=True)` after new games are played.
- Older seasons, and what was done about each difference (a new one belongs in the same places: `load` for names, `depth.py` for depth charts, `_Rosters.players` for a player's fields):
  - Team codes: only the play-by-play uses the current ones for every season. `load` renames the rest (`TEAMS`).
  - Roster positions before 2016 are detailed (`T`, `DE`, `CB`); `GROUPS` maps them. `years_exp` is missing for a few 2016 rows.
  - The weekly depth charts (before 2025) have wrong rows: a quarterback at left guard, the kicker and punter swapped. They are kept as published; the baseline lineups predictor doesn't trust a slot over a position for linemen and specialists.
  - A roster sometimes lists the kicker as a punter (the 2022 and early 2023 Chargers). A team with two punters and no kicker gets the depth chart's kicker as its `K`.
  - Participation rows before 2023 list `players_on_play` in another id system, and personnel without the quarterback and linemen (`1 RB, 1 TE, 3 WR`); `shared.priors.played` and `personnel` read both forms. About a quarter of those seasons' rows have no personnel.
  - 2016's week 1 rosters hold up to 90 players a team (not yet cut down), so those contexts carry extras.
  - A team can really have no kicker (the 2015 Jets, week 9: the punter kicked). The context is kept as it is and fails `validate_context`; its field goal plays can't be simulated.
  - A few roster files miss linemen (the 2011 Rams, week 1, have four), and their plays fail in simulation.
